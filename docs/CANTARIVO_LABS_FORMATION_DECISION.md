## Recommended starting structure

For the current private-pilot stage, the conservative working recommendation is:

1. Form **Cantarivo Labs LLC in the state where the business is actually operated**, unless startup counsel identifies a concrete financing reason to form elsewhere.
2. Use the LLC's default federal tax classification initially: disregarded entity for one eligible owner or partnership for multiple owners.
3. Do **not** make an S-corporation, C-corporation, or trust-ownership election until a CPA and business attorney review the ownership, twelve-month profit forecast, payroll, fundraising plan, state taxes, and estate plan.
4. Revisit an S election when the business has stable profit above a supportable reasonable owner salary and enough savings to justify payroll and additional filings.
5. If institutional investment or multiple stock classes become an actual plan, compare forming or converting to **Cantarivo Labs, Inc.**, taxed as a C corporation, before issuing interests or signing investment documents.

## Why no trust percentage split is drafted

An S corporation can have only allowable shareholders, no more than 100 shareholders, and one class of stock. Only certain trusts qualify. S-corporation income is generally allocated per share and per day, and shareholders can owe tax on their share even when cash is retained in the company. A custom percentage split of tax liability separate from economic ownership is therefore not a safe S-corporation planning mechanism.

A trust should be created for a genuine estate, asset-management, or beneficiary purpose by counsel who can determine whether it is a grantor trust, QSST, ESBT, or another eligible form. An ineligible trust or missed election can terminate S status. Do not transfer company interests to a trust before that review.

A C corporation currently computes regular federal corporate tax at 21 percent and can retain after-tax earnings for documented business needs, but dividends can create a second shareholder-level tax and unreasonable accumulations may implicate the accumulated earnings tax. That tradeoff requires financial projections rather than a general assumption that retained earnings automatically save tax.

## Information required before filing

- Formation state and every state where the business will actually operate
- Principal office and mailing address
- Registered agent and registered-office consent
- Organizer name
- Every owner/member's legal name, contribution, voting rights, and percentage
- Member-managed or manager-managed structure
- Effective date and fiscal year
- Business purpose and initial capitalization
- Whether any owner is a nonresident alien or another entity
- The trust's purpose, governing state, trustee, beneficiaries, tax status, and counsel's S-shareholder eligibility opinion, if trust ownership is still desired
- Twelve-month revenue, expense, profit, owner-service, salary, distribution, and reinvestment forecast
- Fundraising plan and whether investors will require preferred stock

Do not place Social Security numbers, trust documents, bank details, or identity documents in this repository or a public issue. Provide them only through the filing agency's secure process or retained counsel/CPA.

## Filing sequence after approval

1. State name search/reservation if useful.
2. State formation filing and acceptance.
3. Operating agreement reflecting actual ownership and governance.
4. EIN application with the IRS.
5. Business bank account and accounting/payroll setup.
6. State and local tax, licensing, and foreign-qualification review.
7. Beneficial-ownership reporting review under the rules effective on the actual filing date.
8. CPA election memo. File Form 2553 only for an approved S election; use Form 8832 when an eligible LLC intentionally elects another corporate classification and the tax adviser confirms it is required.
9. Written IP assignment or license covering the Cantarivo code, brand, domains, accounts, audio, and contracts.
10. Update the privacy policy, Play Console developer account, support contacts, contracts, copyright notices, and repository only after the entity exists and owns or is licensed to use those assets.
